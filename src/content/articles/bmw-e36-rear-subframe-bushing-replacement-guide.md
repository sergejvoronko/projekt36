## TL;DR

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| **What:** | Replacing the bushings of the **final drive carrier**: the rear subframe that carries the differential and the rear suspension arms. |
| **Why:** | Worn carrier bushings let the whole rear axle move: clunks on take-off and gear changes, vagueness, a rear end that feels like it's steering itself. |
| **How:** | Bentley's procedure lowers the carrier on a transmission jack after removing the driveshaft, drive axles and suspension arms. A purpose-made bushing press is needed for the bushings themselves. |
| **Difficulty:** | 4/5 |

## Symptoms

- **Clunk** from the rear when taking off, changing gear or lifting off
- **Rear-end vagueness:** the car feels as if the rear steers itself under acceleration or braking
- **Wheel hop** under hard launches

Worn carrier bushings also let more movement and load reach the body mounting points. On hard-driven cars, inspect the floor around the mounts for cracks while everything is apart.

## Bushing choices

| Material | Character | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Factory-style rubber | Best isolation, factory ride | Daily drivers, restorations |
| Polyurethane | Much less axle movement; more noise and vibration, including more differential whine in the cabin | Fast road use, occasional track |
| Solid (aluminium or similar) | No compliance, harsh and noisy, higher loads into the body | Race and drift cars |

Part numbers depend on the model: look them up by VIN on [RealOEM](https://www.realoem.com/).

## Removing the final drive carrier (Bentley)

Work with the car **securely on jack stands at structural points**, wheels off.

1. **Driveshaft:** remove it (mark its orientation to the flange first).
2. **Drive axles:** remove both.
3. **Anti-roll bar:** disconnect it from the upper control arms and remove its mounts.
4. **Speedometer connector** on the final drive: disconnect it, where fitted.
5. **Exhaust:** remove the rear section. Removing the complete exhaust as one piece may be easier.
6. **Control arms:** remove the upper and lower control arms. Releasing the coil spring means lowering the trailing arm. **Bentley recommends a retaining chain between the spring's bottom coil and the upper control arm**, and releasing the spring **slowly**: a compressed spring can cause injury.
7. **Support the carrier** with a transmission jack, remove the mounting bolts and nuts at the carrier bushing mounts, and **lower the final drive and carrier slowly**, detaching wiring as you go.
8. The final drive can then be unbolted from the carrier.

### Bushings

Press the old bushings out and the new ones in with a **purpose-made bushing press**. Clean the bores first, and press the new bushings in **straight**. Follow the bushing maker's instructions: polyurethane bushings usually go in differently from one-piece rubber ones.

### While it's down: reinforcement

With the carrier out, the body mounting points are fully exposed. This is the moment to inspect them for cracks and, on hard-driven cars, to fit **weld-in reinforcement plates**. That is a job for a competent welder. See the [chassis reinforcement guide](/guides/bmw-e36-chassis-reinforcement-subframe-plates-guide).

## Installation (Bentley)

- Installation is the reverse of removal.
- **Always use new self-locking fasteners.**
- Tighten rubber-bushed joints **with the car at normal loaded ride height**.
- **Check and adjust the parking brake.**
- **Have the car professionally aligned.**

| Fastener | Torque |
|---|---|
| Final drive carrier to body | 77 Nm |
| Final drive to final drive carrier (M12), front mount | 95 Nm |
| Final drive to final drive carrier (M12), rear mounts | 77 Nm |
| Upper or lower control arm to final drive carrier (M12) | 77 Nm |
| Upper or lower control arm to trailing arm (M12) | 110 Nm |
| Drive axle to final drive flange, M8 / M10 Torx | 64 / 100 Nm |
| Shock absorber to trailing arm (car at loaded ride height) | 77 Nm |
| Driveshaft to final drive flange, U-joint (M10 ribbed / compressed nut) | 80 / 60 Nm |
| Driveshaft to final drive flange, CV joint (M8) | 32 Nm |
| Road wheel to hub | 100 ± 10 Nm |
