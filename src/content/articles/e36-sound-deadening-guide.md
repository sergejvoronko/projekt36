## TL;DR

- Sound deadening uses **three different materials** for three different jobs: **damping mats** (stop panels ringing), a **mass barrier** (blocks airborne noise) and **closed-cell foam** (decouples and absorbs).
- **Damping mats don't need full coverage:** a partial patch in the middle of each large flat panel stops it resonating. A **mass barrier only works with near-full coverage**, with no gaps.
- **Do it with the shell stripped**, before the wiring and interior go back in, and **keep drain holes, harness routes and service openings clear**.

---

## Why bother on an E36

A 25-plus-year-old E36 has tired factory insulation, rattling trim and drier seals. On the Projekt 36 car the swap from the four-cylinder M43 to the six-cylinder M50 will also change how the engine and exhaust sound in the cabin. Sound deadening isn't about silence: it's about removing the drone, rattles and boom that make a long drive tiring.

---

## The three materials

### 1. Damping mats (constrained-layer damping, CLD)

A butyl layer with an aluminium foil top, rolled onto the metal. It adds mass and damping to a panel so it stops ringing. Tap an untreated door skin and you hear it ring; with a patch of damping mat it just thuds.

- **Where:** large flat panels: door outer skins, floor, boot floor, roof, rear quarter panels, spare wheel well.
- **How much:** partial coverage of the centre of each panel is the usual practice. Covering every square centimetre adds weight for little extra benefit.
- **Choose:** butyl-based mats from established car-audio brands. Avoid asphalt-based mats, which can smell and soften in summer heat.

### 2. Mass barrier (mass-loaded vinyl, MLV)

A heavy, flexible sheet that blocks airborne noise (road, exhaust, engine). It works only as a **continuous layer**: gaps let the noise through. It goes on **top of a foam decoupling layer**, not straight onto the metal.

- **Where:** floor and transmission tunnel, bulkhead (cabin side), boot floor.
- **Trade-off:** it's the heaviest part of the system. For a road car the comfort is usually worth it; for a track car, skip it.

### 3. Closed-cell foam

Decouples the mass barrier from the metal, stops trim rattling against panels and absorbs some sound.

- **Use closed-cell only.** Open-cell foam soaks up water and holds it against the floor: a direct route to rust.
- **Where:** under the mass barrier; behind door cards and trim panels; between plastic trim and metal.

## Where to put what in an E36 sedan

| Area | Damping mat | Foam | Mass barrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front floor and tunnel | Centre of the flat sections | Full layer | Full layer |
| Bulkhead (cabin side) | Where accessible | Full layer | Full layer, as far as you can reach |
| Doors | Outer skin, centre; patch the large openings in the inner skin | Behind the door card | Optional |
| Rear floor, under the seat | Centre of the flat sections | Full layer | Full layer |
| Boot floor and spare wheel well | Centre of the flat sections | Over the floor | Optional |
| Roof | Centre sections between the bows | Thin layer | Not usually |
| Rear quarter panels and parcel shelf | Centre sections | Behind trim | Optional |

---

## Keep these clear

- **Drain holes** in the floor and door bottoms. Water must still be able to get out.
- **The door's water path:** the outer door skin gets wet on the inside, which is normal. Keep mats away from the bottom edge and drain holes, and **renew the vapour barrier** on the inner door skin (see the [door panel guide](/guides/bmw-e36-door-panel-restoration-guide)).
- **Wiring routes and grommets**, so the harness still fits.
- **Service openings:** fuel pump access under the rear seat, sender access, and any bolts you'll need again.
- **Hot areas:** near the exhaust tunnel, use materials rated for the temperature.

---

## Application tips

1. **Clean and degrease** every panel; the butyl only sticks to clean metal.
2. **Work warm.** In a cold garage, warm the mats and panels so the butyl bonds.
3. **Roll every mat down firmly** with a hard roller, especially the edges. Air bubbles under the foil reduce the effect.
4. **Treat rust first.** Never cover rust or bare metal: weld, prime and seal it before deadening goes on. See the [rust map](/guides/e36-rust-map-every-spot).

## When it fits in the Projekt 36 build

The plan is to fit sound deadening **after the metalwork and rust repair** and **before the new wiring harness, the air ride installation and the interior go back in**:

1. Teardown
2. Metalwork and rust repair
3. **Sound deadening** (bare shell, everything accessible)
4. Wiring for the M50 swap
5. Air ride: the tank and compressor go in the boot, so the boot layout is planned **before** any deadening there
6. Interior reassembly

Before-and-after noise measurements will be documented in the build journal when the work is done.
