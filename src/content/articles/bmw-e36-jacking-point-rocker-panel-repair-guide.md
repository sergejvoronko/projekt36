## TL;DR

- **What:** Cutting out and rebuilding rusted E36 jacking points, inner jack pad tubes, and outer rocker panels.
- **Why:** Restores structural integrity, prevents the chassis from collapsing on a two-post lift, and is required to pass strict technical inspections (TÜV/MOT).
- **Cost:** €150 – €400 per side (depending on repair panel quality and rust prevention chemicals).
- **Time:** 10 – 20 hours per corner.
- **Difficulty:** 4/5 (Requires MIG welding, sheet metal fabrication, and precise structural alignment).

## The E36 Jacking Point Iceberg: Understanding the Rot

If you’ve owned an E36 for more than five minutes, you know the sound: the sickening crunch of a two-post lift arm pushing directly into the cabin because the jacking point has disintegrated. The E36 jacking point is notorious for rust, and what you see on the outside is rarely the full story. 

The OEM design relies on a cylindrical steel tube that acts as the lifting socket. This tube is welded to a heavy-gauge inner reinforcement plate, which ties into the floor pan and the inner sill. The outer rocker panel (the sill skin) merely covers this assembly. 

When the plastic/rubber jack pads (`51711960752`) fall out, which they almost always do, the hollow tube becomes a scoop for water, salt, and road debris. Combined with clogged sunroof drains that dump water directly into the rocker cavity, the inner structure rots from the inside out. By the time you see bubbling on the outer sill, the heavy-gauge inner support structure is usually completely gone. You cannot simply weld a patch over the outer rocker; lifting the car will instantly crush the patch. You must rebuild the inner structural node.

## Parts, Panels, and Materials

While you can fabricate everything from flat sheet metal, buying pre-made repair panels saves dozens of hours. Companies like Valcas Garage and various eBay fabricators now sell heavy-duty CNC-cut jacking point repair panels that replicate the inner tube and support plate. For the outer skin, Klokkerholm is the standard aftermarket choice.

## Surgical Assessment and Cutting

Before firing up the angle grinder, support the car on its suspension or subframe mounting points. **Do not support the car by the sills or floor pans while cutting out structural nodes.** 

Start by stripping the side skirts, interior carpet edges, and lower trim. The heat from welding will ignite the sound deadening under the carpet if you leave it in place. 

1. **Map the Cuts:** Use a wire wheel to strip the paint and undercoating around the rusted area. Find clean, solid metal. Do not attempt to weld to pitted or thinned steel. 
2. **Remove the Outer Skin:** Cut a rectangular window in the outer rocker panel. It's usually best to cut just below the upper feature line of the sill to make bodywork easier later. 
3. **Drill the Spot Welds:** Drill out the factory spot welds along the bottom pinch weld (where the outer sill meets the inner sill and floor pan).
4. **Excavate the Inner Structure:** Once the outer skin is off, you will see the remains of the jack tube. Cut away the rotted inner reinforcement plate. Be extremely careful not to cut into the primary inner sill structure unless it is also compromised. 

A small bubble on the rocker can hide far larger rot behind it, in the front of the floor pan and the base of the A-pillar. Be prepared for scope creep.

## Rebuilding the Inner Structural Node

The jack pad tube transfers the entire weight of that corner of the car directly into the inner sill and floor pan. This area must be incredibly strong.

If you are using a pre-fabricated inner repair kit (like the Valcas units), alignment is your biggest challenge. The tube must perfectly align with the hole in your new outer rocker panel, and it must sit flush with the bottom pinch weld so the OEM rubber puck can snap in securely.

1. **Reconstruct the Floor Pan:** If the floor where the jack plate mounts is weak, cut it back to clean metal and weld in a 1.5mm or 2mm patch. Butt weld this patch for maximum strength and grind it flush.
2. **Position the Jack Tube:** Clamp the new inner jack tube/plate assembly to the inner sill. Temporarily clamp your outer rocker repair panel over it to ensure the tube protrudes exactly to the edge of the outer hole. 
3. **Weld the Inner Node:** Once aligned, remove the outer skin. Weld the inner plate securely to the floor pan and inner sill. Use a combination of plug welds (drilled at 8mm or 10mm) and solid seam welds. Ensure excellent penetration, run your MIG welder relatively hot here since you are joining thick gauge steel.
4. **Prime the Cavity:** Clean the newly welded inner structure. Coat everything generously with 2K epoxy primer. Once cured, spray the mating surfaces and the inside of the cavity with a high-quality zinc weld-through primer where the outer skin will attach.

## Fitting and Welding the Outer Rocker Panel

Replacement outer sills from Klokkerholm or similar suppliers rarely fit perfectly out of the box. They are stamped for mass production and will require trimming and finessing.

Instead of replacing the entire sill if you only have local damage, cut a patch from the replacement panel that overlaps your cutout by 1-2 inches. 

1. **Trace and Trim:** Lay the patch over the hole, scribe a highly accurate line on the car's body, and use a 1mm cutting disc to trim the car’s sill to match the patch perfectly. You are aiming for a zero-gap butt weld. Avoid lap welding, as it creates a moisture trap behind the panel.
2. **Tack Welding:** Hold the panel flush using butt-welding clamps or magnets. Place a tack weld every 2 to 3 inches. 
3. **Manage the Heat:** The E36 outer rocker is relatively thin sheet metal and will warp easily. Jump around the panel, placing one tack at a time, moving as far away from the previous tack as possible. Use compressed air to cool the welds immediately. 
4. **The Pinch Weld:** Along the bottom edge, you will plug weld the outer sill to the inner sill and floor pan flange. Drill 8mm holes every 1.5 inches along the bottom lip of your repair panel and fill them with the MIG welder, tying all three layers together.
5. **Grinding:** Carefully grind the butt welds flush using a flap disc. Be careful not to thin out the surrounding metal.

## Sealing, Paint, and Cavity Protection

Welding is only 50% of the job. If you fail to seal the repair properly, the E36 will rust out in the exact same spot within two winters. 

**Exterior Protection:**
Wipe the bare metal with silicone remover. Apply two coats of a true 2K epoxy primer (like Mipa EP 100-20). Do not use rattle-can 1K primer; it is porous and absorbs moisture. Once the epoxy is cured, apply body filler to smooth out the butt welds, block sand it, and follow up with a high-build primer. Coat the pinch welds in a brushable automotive seam sealer to prevent water ingress between the panels. Finally, apply a heavy stone-chip / underbody coating (Schutz) to the lower sill before applying the final body color. 

**Internal Cavity Wax (The Most Critical Step):**
You have just introduced new welds inside a dark, unventilated cavity. Condensation will form on the back of these welds immediately. Use a 360-degree spray wand to flood the internal rocker cavity with a creeping rust preventative. Fluid Film AS-R is excellent for immediate penetration, while a heavier wax like Mike Sanders will provide permanent, long-term protection. Spray heavily down the A-pillar, B-pillar, and through the access plugs in the wheel wells.

**Installing the Puck:**
Before snapping the new OEM rubber jack pad (`51711960752`) into the newly fabricated tube, coat the inside of the tube and the shaft of the rubber pad with thick marine grease or waterproof anti-seize. This ensures the pad seals out moisture and doesn't trap water against the fresh steel.

## What's Next

With the structural integrity of your jacking points restored, your E36 is finally safe to put on a two-post lift without fear of the chassis collapsing. If you're building a track car or conducting a full restoration, this is the perfect time to inspect the rear trailing arm pocket (RTAB) mounting points and the rear subframe mounts, as they suffer from similar fatigue and corrosion issues. If your outer sills are looking somewhat scarred from the repair process, installing a set of OEM M-Technic side skirts is the period-correct way to protect your fresh metal and upgrade the exterior aesthetics.
