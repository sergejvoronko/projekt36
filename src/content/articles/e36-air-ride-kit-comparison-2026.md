## TL;DR

- The E36 never had air suspension from the factory: every system is aftermarket. It replaces the springs and dampers with **air struts and air springs**, and adds a **compressor, tank, valves and management**.
- **Management is the big choice:** **pressure-based** systems hold a set pressure per corner; **height-based** systems use sensors to hold a set ride height.
- **Verified E36 options:** Air Lift Performance kits with 3P (pressure) or 3H (height + pressure) management; Bag Riders kits for the E36; and EU suppliers such as Brabant Custom in the Netherlands, who sell E36 bolt-on kits and avoid import from outside the EU.
- **Prices change constantly:** get current quotes, including shipping, duties and VAT.

## How an E36 air system is built

| Component | Job |
|---|---|
| Air struts (front) and air springs with dampers (rear) | Carry the car on air; dampers control the movement |
| Compressor | Fills the tank |
| Tank | Stores air, so height changes are quick |
| Valves and management | Route air to each corner, with presets and control from a controller or phone |
| Air lines and fittings | Connect it all; quality and routing decide reliability |

The compressor and tank usually go in the boot. Plan the boot layout **before** any boot sound deadening or trim work, so nothing has to be stuck down around them later. See the [sound deadening guide](/guides/e36-sound-deadening-guide).

---

## Management: pressure or height?

- **Pressure-based (e.g. Air Lift 3P):** you set a pressure per corner and the system holds it. Simple and reliable. The ride height changes a little with load and temperature.
- **Height-based (e.g. Air Lift 3H):** height sensors at each corner let the system hold a ride height, adjusting the pressure as load changes. More precise, with more parts to install.

Both types offer presets, such as driving height, low parking height and a raised height for ramps.

## Before you buy

1. **Fitment:** the exact body (sedan, coupé, Touring, Compact) and front strut type. The Compact uses a different rear suspension and its own kits.
2. **Damping adjustment:** air springs replace the coil springs, but the dampers still decide how the car rides.
3. **Management and parts:** what's included and what costs extra (compressor, tank, sensors, controller).
4. **Installation:** lines away from heat and moving parts, a properly fused power supply, and tight fittings. Leaks are the most common air-ride problem.
5. **Registration and inspection:** in Slovakia, as across much of the EU, suspension changes may need approval. Ask the inspection authority **before** buying.
6. **Alignment:** any new suspension, and every ride-height change you drive on regularly, needs an alignment. See the [geometry guide](/guides/bmw-e36-suspension-geometry-setup-guide).
