## TL;DR

- **Oil pressure (Bentley):** at least **0.5 bar at idle**; regulated pressure on the six-cylinder **4.0 bar** at elevated engine speed.
- **Capacity:** **6.5 litres** with a filter change (M50 and M52).
- **Low pressure** means a worn pump, worn bearings or severe engine wear: all major repairs. Test with a gauge before deciding anything.
- **Oil pan removal** on cars built after September 1992 requires supporting the engine from above and lowering the front suspension crossmember.

---

## How it works

On the six-cylinder, the oil pump is mounted to the engine block in the oil pan area and **driven by a chain and sprocket**; the sprocket nut has a **left-hand thread**. The pump draws oil through a **pickup tube** in the pan and feeds the engine through the filter in the **oil filter housing**, which also carries the **oil pressure switch**.

---

## Checking the warning light and switch (Bentley)

1. **Ignition on, engine off:** the oil pressure warning light must come on.
2. **Unplug the switch:** the light must go out. If it stays on, the wire to the switch is shorted to ground somewhere.
3. **If the light doesn't come on with the ignition on:** ground the switch connector with a jumper wire. If the light then comes on, test the switch. If not, the fault is in the wiring to the cluster or the bulb.
4. **The switch itself:** an ohmmeter between its terminal and ground should show **continuity with the engine off** and **no continuity with the engine running**, when oil pressure opens it.

If the light stays on with the engine running, **check the oil pressure and don't drive the car** until the cause is found: the engine may be severely damaged.

---

## Measuring oil pressure

1. Check the oil level first. For the most accurate result, the oil and filter should be new and the correct grade.
2. Remove the **oil pressure switch** from the oil filter housing (some oil will drain) and fit a pressure gauge in its place.
3. Start the engine and check the pressure **cold and hot**.

| Condition | Specification (Bentley) |
|---|---|
| Idle, minimum | 0.5 bar |
| Regulated pressure at elevated engine speed (six-cylinder) | 4.0 bar |

**Low pressure** points to a worn or faulty oil pump, worn engine bearings or severe engine wear. All of these need major repairs, so confirm the measurement with a good gauge before tearing the engine down. Thicker oil isn't a fix.

## Capacity and oil

| Engine | Oil with filter change (Bentley) |
|---|---|
| M50 | 6.5 L |
| M52 | 6.5 L |
| S50US | 6.5 L |
| S52US | 6.0 L |

Use the grade and specification BMW lists for your car, and change the oil regularly: an old engine of unknown history deserves shorter intervals rather than longer ones.

## Oil pan removal (six-cylinder)

- **Cars built after 9/92** have a different front suspension crossmember. Removing the oil pan requires **supporting the engine from above** and **unbolting and lowering the front crossmember**.
- The **dipstick guide tube** is sealed with an **O-ring**: check it comes out with the tube, and fit a new one.
- The **oil pickup** is involved in the job; Bentley notes the pickup tube is refitted with a **new gasket**.

**Refitting:**

- Clean every sealing surface completely and fit a **new pan gasket**.
- Apply a bead of **sealer** (3-Bond 1209 or equivalent, about **3 mm wide and 2 mm high**) at the **joints** where the pan gasket meets the end cover and the front timing case cover.
- Tighten the **front pan bolts first**, then the rear.
- Fill with oil, start the engine and raise it to about **2,500 rpm until the oil pressure light goes out** (about five seconds).

## Oil pump

The six-cylinder pump comes out with the oil pan off: remove the sprocket nut (**left-hand thread**) and lift the sprocket off with its drive chain, unbolt the pickup tube from the pump, then unbolt the pump from the block.

| Fastener | Torque (Bentley) |
|---|---|
| Oil pump to engine block (M8) | 22 Nm |
| Oil pump sprocket nut (M10×1, left-hand thread) | 25 Nm |
| Oil pan to block, M6 grade 8.8 / 10.9 | 10 / 12 Nm |
| Oil drain plug, M12 / M22 | 25 / 60 Nm |
| Oil filter cover (M8) | 25 Nm |

Inspect the pickup screen while you're in there: sludge or debris on it says a lot about the engine's past, and pieces of black plastic point to the timing chain guides. See the [timing chain guide](/guides/e36-m50-timing-chain-guide).

## Hard driving and track use

The standard pan is designed for road use. For sustained hard cornering or track days, owners commonly fit a baffled oil pan or an oil accumulator to keep the pickup covered. Whatever you fit, keep the oil level at the full mark, and check the pressure with a gauge after any change to the system.
