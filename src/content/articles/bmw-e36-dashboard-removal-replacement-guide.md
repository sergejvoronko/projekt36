## Safety: airbags

- **Disconnect the negative battery cable and insulate the terminal** before starting.
- Bentley says to **wait 10 minutes** after disconnecting the battery before working on airbag parts, so the SRS module's reserve power can discharge.
- **Store removed airbags facing up** (horn pad or cover up) in a safe place. Bentley warns that an airbag stored face down can be thrown violently if it deploys by accident.
- Once the steering wheel is off, **don't turn the airbag contact reel**. It locks in its centre position and must stay there.

## Removal sequence (Bentley)

1. **Battery:** disconnect the negative cable.
2. **Centre console** out.
3. **Glove compartment** out.
4. **Lower dash trim panels**, left and right.
5. **Driver and passenger airbags** out (see above).
6. **Steering wheel:** wheels straight ahead; remove the centre bolt, **mark the wheel's position** on the column shaft, turn the ignition on to unlock the steering, and pull the wheel off.
7. **Driver's knee bolster.**
8. **Instrument cluster.**
9. **Switches:** headlight switch, turn-signal and wiper stalks.
10. **Heating/AC control head and module.**
11. **Radio.**
12. **A-pillar trims**, both sides.
13. **Steering column:** remove the reinforcement bar below the column and the column **shear (headless) bolts**. They have to be **drilled out** and replaced with new ones on reassembly. Lower the column **only slightly** and support it: Bentley warns that lowering it too far damages the column supports.
14. **Glove compartment support frame.**
15. **Defroster outlets** at the base of the windscreen: pry them out and remove the screw below each.
16. **Mounting bolts** at the left and right ends of the dashboard (one or two each side, depending on the model).
17. **Lift the dashboard out slowly**, checking that every connector and harness is disconnected.

Label every connector and bag every set of screws as they come out. Photos at each step make reassembly much easier.

## While it's out

- **Wiring:** remove bad previous-owner wiring (alarms, audio), re-wrap brittle harness tape with cloth harness tape, and run any new wiring (gauges, audio) now.
- **Heater box flap foam:** if bits of foam blow out of the vents, the flap seals have broken down. With the dash out, the heater box is much easier to reach.
- **Heater core:** you can replace it now too, although it doesn't require dash removal. Part numbers by VIN on [RealOEM](https://www.realoem.com/) (*Heater and air conditioning*).
- **Sound deadening** on the bulkhead and front floor. See the [sound deadening guide](/guides/e36-sound-deadening-guide).

## Replacement options for a cracked dash

- **A good used dashboard:** the usual route. Check carefully for fine cracks, especially around the passenger airbag cover and the defroster vents, and make sure it matches your car's airbag configuration.
- **Flocking:** a matt, glare-free finish applied by a specialist.
- **Re-trimming:** covered in leather, vinyl or Alcantara by a trim shop.

Transfer vents, clips and brackets to the replacement on the bench before fitting it.

## Refitting

Installation is the reverse of removal (Bentley), with these points:

- **New steering column shear bolts.**
- **Steering wheel:** align your marks, make sure the **airbag contact reel's locking pin engages its cutout**, and don't over-tighten the centre bolt. Use the torque in your workshop manual.
- **Airbags:** fit them as Bentley's airbag section describes.
- **Reconnect every connector as you go**, and test the cluster and the heating controls before the trim goes back on.
- **Reconnect the battery last.** With the ignition on, the **airbag warning light** must go out after its self-test. If it stays on, the fault must be found and cleared before the car is driven.
