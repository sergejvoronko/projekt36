PROJEKT36 - MACRO HERO PROMPTS (replaces the garage-lock set in hero_prompts.txt)
=================================================================================

GOAL
One consistent look for every article hero: a close-up / macro still life of a
single generic part or material on a dark workshop bench. The wide garage and car
shots (the current heroes) move INTO the articles as inline photos.

HOW TO USE
1. Prepend STYLE BLOCK and append NEGATIVES to every SCENE below.
2. Output 16:9, 1344x768, saved under the filename in each "# name.webp" line.
3. Do NOT send the garage reference photos. Where a prompt says REF:, send a real
   photo of that part (yours, or a supplier photo) as the only image input.
4. Generate 3 variants per scene, keep the best. Reject any frame with a recognisable
   assembly that looks wrong, extra or fused bolts, readable text, or a hand.
5. After replacing heroes, regenerate public/heroes/thumb/ (480x275, centre crop).


STYLE BLOCK (identical on every prompt, do not edit per scene)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Macro product photography of a single subject on a worn dark steel workshop bench.
Shot on a 100mm macro lens at f/2.8, very shallow depth of field, subject sharp
at the front edge only. One warm amber key light from the side (about 3000K) with
a faint cool blue rim light behind. Heavy darkness at the frame edges, soft
out-of-focus workshop background with a single blurred amber light bokeh.
Real-world wear: fine scratches on the bench, a thin film of oil, light dust,
honest metal texture. Muted colour grade, deep blacks, subtle film grain.
Subject placed in the lower-left third, empty dark space top-right (room for
the dark gradient overlay and title crop). 16:9.


NEGATIVES (append to every prompt)
----------------------------------
No text, letters, numbers, logos or BMW roundel. No people, hands or tools in
frame unless the scene says so. No whole car, no whole engine, no complete
assemblies. No glowing or neon effects, no sparks, no smoke, no CGI gloss,
no symmetrical showroom arrangement, no pristine unused parts.


RULES THAT KEEP IT LOOKING REAL
-------------------------------
- Subject is ONE part or a small loose group (max 3 items), never a built-up assembly.
- Prefer shapes with no single correct form: gears, rings, bolts, chain, gaskets,
  bushings, hose, wire, foam, leather, paint, rust, glass. Avoid anything where the
  exact M50/M52 casting matters (manifolds, VANOS housings, ECUs, full heads).
- Where the article is about a complex part, show a DETAIL of it (seal, bolt head,
  gasket edge, connector pin) rather than the part as a whole.
- Keep fastener counts low and let background items stay out of focus.
